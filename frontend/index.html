Swappers, liquidity providers, and merchants — all trustless, all peer-to-peer.

Swappers

Swap XMR for wsXMR and back — like an instant exchange, but trustless. No custodian, no KYC, no counterparty risk. Once you hold wsXMR, trade it for any token on any DEX across EVM.

Features

Protocol mechanics

The moving parts that make Wrapsynth work — configurable, on-chain, verifiable.

Mint fees & burn rewards

LPs charge a configurable mint fee in basis points. Burners receive a burn reward, also in bps, incentivizing the burn flow that releases XMR back to users. Both are set per-vault and visible on-chain.

Configurable bpsPer-vaultOn-chain

Yield war chest & buy-and-burn

Excess sDAI yield from vaults accrues in an on-chain war chest. When XMR dips below its EMA, anyone can trigger a buy-and-burn — the chest redeems sDAI, buys wsXMR on Uniswap V3, and burns it. This reduces outstanding debt and tightens the collateral ratio for all holders.

sDAI yieldUniswap V3Dip-triggeredKeeper reward

CDP co-LP system

Vaults can accept co-liquidity providers who deposit sDAI alongside the primary LP. Co-LPs share in fees proportionally, with a configurable range (maxCoLPRangeBps) capping their share of the vault.

Co-LP depositsProportional feesRange-limited

Overcollateralized CDP

Every wsXMR is backed by >200% collateral at mint time. A 120% liquidation threshold and 10% liquidator bonus keep the system solvent. Real-time price feeds from Pyth and RedStone trigger automated liquidation.

>200% ratio120% threshold10% bonus

Atomic swap escrow

XMR is held in a 2-of-2 Monero multisig until the swap completes or times out (24h max). No party has unilateral custody. The secret-reveal mechanism ensures atomicity — either both sides settle or neither does.

2-of-2 multisig24h timeoutNo custodian

On-chain Ed25519 verification

Secret commitments are verified on-chain using Ed25519 operations — the same curve Monero uses. The contract checks that the revealed secret matches the commitment before minting or releasing funds. No off-chain oracle needed.

Ed25519Monero-compatibleNo oracle

How Wrapsynth came to be

Click a point to explore.

2009
Bitcoin launches
Genesis block: "Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks." Trustless electronic cash — no intermediaries, no gatekeepers. The original promise.
2013
Mt. Gox handles 70% of Bitcoin trading
The promise of self-sovereign money, funneled through a single point of failure. 850,000 BTC would be lost when it collapsed in February 2014. The lesson was already clear: centralization is the weakness.
April 2014
Monero is born
From the CryptoNote whitepaper. Private by default — every transaction hides sender, recipient, and amount. No rich list. No traceable history. The spiritual successor to Bitcoin's original promise.
2017
The Bitcoin Block Size War
Bitcoin grinds to a halt. 1MB cap, $50 fees, transactions stuck for days. The community fractures into BTC and BCH. Years of toxic debate over a parameter that should have been uncontroversial. Meanwhile Monero has dynamic block sizes — the median scales automatically with usage. No cap to fight over. No civil war.
2016 — 2022
Monero hardens
While exchanges prepared to delist it, Monero was getting stronger.
RingCT
Transaction amounts hidden cryptographically. No one can see how much is being sent — not the sender, not the recipient, not the network.
RandomX + P2Pool
ASIC-resistant mining so a CPU competes with a data center. Trustless decentralized mining pool so no operator can withhold payouts or censor miners.
Dandelion++
Transaction propagation that hides your IP from the network. Even your ISP can't easily link you to your transactions.
Tail Emission
June 2022, block 2,641,623: reward locks at 0.6 XMR forever. Miners always have a reason to secure the network. No supply cliff.
2021 — 2022
First delistings
Bittrex delists Monero in January 2021, citing "evolving regulatory standards" — no specific law. Huobi follows in September 2022, removing 7 privacy coins at once. Neither exchange names a regulation. The chill begins.
2024
The major delistings
OKX removes all privacy tokens in January. Binance delists Monero in February — XMR drops 30%, then Binance force-converts remaining balances to USDC without consent. LocalMonero, the only P2P fiat on-ramp, shuts down in May after 7 years. Kraken delists for all of Europe in October: "We concluded we have no choice."
2025
73 exchanges drop privacy coins
The delisting wave becomes the new baseline — a 43% increase over 2023. Canadian regulators force Kraken Canada to delist XMR and shut down TradeOgre entirely.
2025 — 2026
Monero survives
On-chain transaction volumes increase, stabilizing above 2020–2021 levels. XMR near $797. Liquidity doesn't disappear — it reroutes. Atomic swaps, DEXs, and wrapped instruments become the new infrastructure.
Today
No exchange can delist what doesn't need an exchange
Wrapsynth brings Monero to DeFi through trustless atomic swaps — no custodian, no multisig, no centralized listing to revoke. Monero's access to DeFi is now permanent.

How

How it works

Four steps to ferry your Monero to DeFi — trustless on both sides of the swap.

Mint wsXMR

1

Initiate swap

You create a mint request with a secret commitment and send XMR to the LP's Monero address.

2

LP confirms receipt

The LP verifies the XMR off-chain and marks the mint request READY on-chain.

3

Reveal secret

You reveal your secret to claim wsXMR — verified on-chain with Ed25519 operations (Monero-compatible).

4

Receive wsXMR

The contract mints wsXMR to your wallet, backed by real XMR and guaranteed by the LP's collateral in their vault.

Burn wsXMR

1

Initiate burn

You create a burn request with a secret commitment and send wsXMR to the contract.

2

LP locks XMR

The LP locks your XMR on Monero with a secret hash and proposes the lock on-chain.

3

Confirm receipt

You verify the Monero transaction and confirm the lock on-chain, binding both parties.

4

Receive XMR

The LP reveals the secret to finalize the burn. Your XMR unlocks and their collateral is released.
